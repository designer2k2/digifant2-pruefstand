The ECU works. In a randomised 12-minute run its injector open time repeated to within 1 µs when the inputs were unchanged, its ignition and injector timing follow the simulated crank speed to 0.01 %, and it reacts to the simulated airflow and coolant signals in the directions a fuel-injection ECU should. The bench does what it was built for, once the three board problems (U6–U8, R10 and the U1 sense trace) are worked around.

What is not established: absolute correctness. There is no known-good ECU or service data to compare against, the sensor voltages are not calibrated to physical units, and four behaviours below still need an explanation.

Defects found

Five problems turned up. Three are corrected in the design files, one is a firmware workaround until the layout changes, and one is waiting on a part.

Analog switches U6–U8 wired COM to NC design fixed

Symptom
VW-9, VW-10 and VW-2 read 0.3 V, 0.4 V and 0.1 V instead of 1.000, 2.500 and 0.450 V. VW-21, which has no switch, was exact.
Cause
On the TS5A3159A in SOT-23-6 the pins are 1 NO, 3 NC, 4 COM. The schematic put the ECU output on pin 3 and left COM, pin 4, open.
Evidence
1.000 V measured on U6 pin 4, so COM follows NO. The KiCad symbol shows pin 4 alone on its side of the chip.
Design
Schematic and PCB corrected in commit 13d5800: pad 4 carries the VW net, pad 3 is open.
This board
A wire from pin 4 to pin 3 on U6, U7 and U8. All four outputs then read exact.

Injector pull-up R10 too weak for the ECU design fixed

Symptom
VW-12 sat at 0.8 V with the ECU running and no injector pulse ever appeared. With the ECU off the line returned to 3.3 V.
Cause
The ECU's injector stage needs real current. 3.3 V through 1 kΩ gives 3.3 mA. The old 12 V test rig (1 kΩ plus an LED) gave about 10 mA and the ECU pulsed normally.
Evidence
A 220 Ω in parallel (about 180 Ω, 18 mA) gave one pulse per crank revolution, 3.5 to 3.9 ms at 850 rpm.
Design
R10 changed to 220 Ω in the source and in the live PCB.
This board
220 Ω from J0 VW-12 to the +3V3 pin of J3.

U1 current sense reads 48 % high workaround

Symptom
U1 reported 0.542 A while the meter in the + lead read 0.367 A.
Cause
U1's IN− sense trace joins the 0.6 mm power trace about 29 mm downstream of RS1 instead of ending on its pad, so it also sees part of that copper's voltage drop.
Evidence
7.32 mV measured across RS1 equals 0.366 A at 0.020 Ω, matching the meter. U1 behaved as if the shunt were 0.0296 Ω.
Design
Logged for the next revision: route IN− and VBUS straight to RS1 pad 2, and check U3 for the same flaw.
This board
Firmware divides by 0.0296 Ω. Afterwards: 0.3587 A on the board, 0.359 A on the meter.

Flux leakage on the idle-switch net cleaned

Symptom
VW-11 measured 45 kΩ to ground with the idle switch off. It should read open.
Cause
Flux residue around Q5 and the J0 pads.
Evidence
Cleaning with isopropyl alcohol raised it to 374 kΩ, negligible against the ECU's pull-up.
Design
No design change.
This board
Cleaned. Measure again after it has dried.

Oscillator Y1 ordered as a 6-pin part blocked

Symptom
The delivered 25 MHz oscillator does not fit the 4-pin footprint.
Cause
The earlier BOM entry named a Renesas family, which is 6-pin, as if it were an IQD part.
Evidence
Checked against the IQD CFPS-73 datasheet: the right part is 4-pin, 7.2 × 5.2 mm.
Design
BOM corrected to LFSPXO018077. Stock still to be confirmed on Mouser.
This board
U4 cannot run without its clock, so the knock-generator tests wait for the right part.

ECU behaviour

Three questions. Are the ECU's outputs stable at a fixed operating point? Do they follow the crank signal exactly? Do they respond to each simulated input in a sensible direction? The sensor voltages are not calibrated to physical units, so direction claims are provisional.

The injector opens a fixed 0.25 ms after the Hall rising edge at every speed (1.3° at 850 rpm, 6.1° at 4000), so the rising edge is the injector's reference.

Response to simulated inputs

Each point holds one input at a new value for 25 s, then averages 6 samples. The other input stays at its baseline. The order was shuffled, with a baseline probe after every third point. Six baseline probes spread over the run agree within 1 µs, so the ECU does not drift while its inputs are unchanged.

What the numbers say

Airflow

The randomised order reproduces the first run at every point (200 mV: 4614 against 4638 µs; 3000 mV: 10 548 against 10 547 µs), so the shape is real. Injector open time is flat at 4.61 ms for 200 and 600 mV, dips to 3.85 ms at 1500 mV, then rises steeply above 2000 mV (5.37, 7.90 and 10.55 ms at 2000, 2500 and 3000 mV, about 5 µs per mV). The spark also retards by about 9° between 2000 and 2500 mV. More airflow giving more fuel and less advance is correct. The flat start and the dip below 1500 mV are not explained. The closed idle contact may select a separate idle map.

Coolant

Injector open time has three regions. At 300 and 800 mV it is 4.09 ms, the same as the baseline, so the ECU may treat that range as out of range and substitute a default. At 1200 and 1500 mV it is 2.6 to 2.7 ms, the lowest. Above that it rises with voltage: 4.71, 5.24 and 5.68 ms at 1800, 2500 and 3300 mV, and the spark advances by about 7° at 3300 mV. A warm engine needing the least fuel and a cold one more is what a coolant sensor should produce. The 2.6 ms at 1200 mV is close to the roughly 2.4 ms idle pulse in your own notes, so 1200 to 1500 mV looks like the right warm-idle range for this bench.

Slow responses

The first run, with 7 s per point, disagreed with this one at 2500 and 3300 mV (3.13 and 3.35 ms against 5.24 and 5.68 ms). The coolant response therefore takes tens of seconds to settle. The airflow response does not: both runs agree. The spark angle is slow too. Twenty-five seconds after the 3000 mV airflow step it was still 4° above its baseline (147.3° against 143.1°), and about 3° above after the 2500 mV step. Allow a minute after a load step before trusting an angle.

Not yet explained

  • The flat start and dip of the airflow curve below 1500 mV.
  • Injector open time does not fall with speed at a constant airflow signal (4.1, 3.8, 3.8, 4.1 and 4.4 ms from 850 to 4000 rpm). If the voltage meant flow, the time should drop roughly as 1/rpm. The ECU may use the voltage as a per-revolution load signal. That run used 7 s settling.
  • No fuel cut-off with the idle contact closed at 2500 rpm: about 85 pulses were counted in the window in both states, at similar widths. Cut-off may need a falling speed or a warm-coolant reading.
  • Closing the idle contact adds 12 mA to the ECU current, which looks like the load of the ECU's own pull-up on VW-11.

Limits

  • There is no known-good ECU or service data. These tests check consistency and plausibility, not conformance.
  • The sensor voltages are not mapped to physical units. That a high coolant voltage means cold is inferred from the ECU's response.
  • The speed sweep and the idle-contact test used 7 s settling, which is short for the slow responses seen later. Their absolute values may move.
  • One ECU, one session, 6 samples per point. Battery voltage and ECU temperature were not controlled.
  • Lambda was held at 450 mV, so any closed-loop trim is untested.
  • The U1 current scale rests on one meter reading. Current figures carry that uncertainty.
  • The spark is taken to be the end of the low phase from the standard ignition principle. It has not been confirmed with a timing light or the knock test.

Next steps

  • Repeat the speed sweep and the idle-contact test with 30 s settling, then ramp the speed down with the idle contact closed to look for fuel cut-off.
  • Measure the coolant step response: jump from 1200 to 3300 mV and log injector time every second for two minutes.
  • Repeat the airflow sweep with the idle contact open to see whether the dip comes from the idle map.
  • Fit the 47 Ω and 22 Ω dummy loads and check the linearity of U1 and the scale of U3.
  • Compare VW-17 with the meter.
  • Order the right oscillator (CFPS-73, LFSPXO018077), fit U4, and run the knock tests, which also settle the spark edge.
  • Next board revision: Kelvin-route the U1 sense lines, and check RS2's. The U6–U8 and R10 fixes are already in the design files.
